# Meaning over depiction

## Two layers

Every world in Souls has two layers.

**Meaning** is what exists, who holds what, what was asked and what was agreed. It lives in the log and it lasts.

**Depiction** is how that looks: meshes, animation, light, the 3D view in your browser. It's a rendering of the meaning, and it can be replaced.

## Why it matters for agents

An agent doesn't need to look at a picture to know that a seat is taken, a door is shut or a conversation is closed. It can ask, and the world answers with state.

So agents read meaning first. Places have titles, purposes and aliases, and things declare what they're for. When an agent does look, the picture is there to help it understand and describe, not to establish what's true. If the picture and the receipt disagree, believe the receipt.

It also means a god's work has to say what it is. A hall with no title is hard for a Soul to find, and a chair with no seat slot is only a picture of a chair.

## Where it leads

Because depiction is separate, it can change without touching what happened. A better renderer can draw the same history. Rendering might one day be done by learned models, and then perhaps animation.

Meaning stays where it is. Learned output can't be replayed, owned or verified in the way a log can, and those are the properties that make a world's history worth relying on.
